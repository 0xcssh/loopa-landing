## The refrigeration question, settled

Rings are refrigerated at the pharmacy for long-term storage before they're dispensed, since cold
storage extends shelf life over months or years sitting on a shelf. Once you have it in hand,
that requirement changes: most rings are stable at room temperature for a defined window, commonly
up to about 4 months, which is far longer than any realistic trip. You don't need a travel cooler
or to worry about a hot car for a few hours. Extreme, sustained heat (leaving it in direct sun in
a parked car for days) isn't a great idea for any medical product, but normal travel conditions
are fine.

## Time zones don't complicate the ring the way they complicate the pill

A daily pill has to be taken within a tight window every 24 hours, which makes crossing several
time zones a genuine logistical question: do you keep your home time, or shift gradually? The
ring doesn't have that problem. Its two events per cycle, insertion and removal, are tracked by
date, not a specific clock hour. Landing in a new time zone doesn't create a missed-dose risk the
way it can for daily methods.

The one thing worth doing: if you're tracking manually, note the date the same way regardless of
which time zone you're currently in, so you don't accidentally recalculate based on local time and
throw off the actual day count.

## What to actually pack

- **The ring you're currently wearing** (or the one you'll insert before departure)
- **At least one spare**, especially for any trip that crosses a scheduled removal or reinsertion
  date. Losing access to a pharmacy mid-trip is the actual risk, not the ring itself failing.
- **If your trip crosses a ring-free week or a change date**, plan for it before you leave rather
  than figuring it out from an unfamiliar pharmacy abroad.

## The actual travel risk isn't the ring, it's losing track of the date
