## First, figure out how late you are

The contraceptive ring is normally worn for 3 weeks, then removed for a break (or replaced
immediately if you're on a continuous schedule). If you've lost track and the ring is still in,
the only number that matters right now is: **how many total days has it been in, since your last
insertion?**

- **21 to 28 days total.** You're late, but the ring is still working. No action needed beyond
  removing it and getting back on schedule.
- **More than 28 days total.** The hormone level has likely dropped below the protective
  threshold. Treat this as a missed dose.

This is the exact rule Loopa's [missed-ring protocol](/en) is built around: it knows which week
you're in and tells you which of these two situations applies, instead of leaving you to do the
math from memory.

## If you're within the 4-week window

Nothing dramatic to do. Remove the ring, and:

- If you follow the standard 3-weeks-in/1-week-out schedule, start your ring-free week as usual.
- If you use continuous (extended) dosing with no break, insert a fresh ring right away.

No backup contraception is required. This buffer exists precisely because ring users forget
sometimes. It's not a loophole, it's built into how the product is designed.

## If you're past the 4-week window

This is the situation that needs action:

1. **Remove the old ring and insert a new one immediately.**
2. **Use condoms, or avoid sex, for the next 7 days.** Your protection needs a week to build back
   up to full effectiveness.
3. **If you had unprotected sex in the days before you remembered**, talk to a pharmacist about
   emergency contraception. It's most effective the sooner it's taken.

## Why this happens more than people admit

The official tracking method for the ring is to remember a weekday, three weeks in the past, and
apply a different catch-up rule depending on which week you're actually in. That's genuinely hard
to hold in memory for months at a time, especially during a busy stretch. It's not a sign you're
bad at this. It's a sign the tracking method itself has no margin for error.

That's the specific gap Loopa closes: instead of a mental calendar, you get a reminder that
repeats until you confirm it, and if you do end up late, a guided next step for the exact week
you're in. No math, no guessing which rule applies.
