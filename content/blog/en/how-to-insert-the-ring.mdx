## The steps

1. **Wash your hands.** Basic hygiene before any vaginal insertion.
2. **Get into a position that's comfortable for you.** Standing with one leg raised on a chair or
   the edge of a tub, squatting, or lying down on your back with knees bent all work. There's no
   required position, whatever lets you relax is the right one.
3. **Squeeze the ring** between your thumb and index finger, compressing it into a narrower oval.
4. **Push it into the vagina**, angling slightly toward your lower back, until it feels
   comfortable. You don't need to reach a specific depth or landmark: once it's far enough that
   you can't feel it, it's in the right place.
5. **Let go.** The ring will unfold and hold its own position. You're done.

## Why there's no wrong spot (mostly)

This is the detail that trips people up coming from other methods: a diaphragm has to sit
precisely against the cervix to work, which makes correct placement genuinely technical. The ring
doesn't work that way. It releases hormones that absorb through the vaginal wall regardless of
its exact position, as long as it's fully inside. The vagina's natural shape holds it in place on
its own, so "did I get it exactly right" isn't really the relevant question. "Can I feel it, and
does it feel secure" is.

## The mistake that actually matters

Perfect insertion technique isn't where the real risk is. The risk is entirely in the schedule:
staying at 3 weeks in (or whatever your specific regimen calls for) and not losing track of when
it went in or needs to come out. A ring inserted slightly off to one side, with a slightly odd
angle, that stays in for the correct 3 weeks works exactly as well as one inserted "perfectly."

## If it feels uncomfortable after insertion

A brief awareness right after inserting is normal and usually settles within a few minutes as it
finds its position. If discomfort continues, try pushing it a little further back; if it still
doesn't feel right after that, it can help to remove and reinsert once, since a slightly different
angle on the second try often resolves it.

## What actually needs tracking

Once it's in, the technique part is done. What matters from here is the same two dates every
cycle: when it needs to come out (or be replaced, for continuous use) and when the next one goes
in. That's the part people actually lose track of, not the insertion itself, which is exactly
