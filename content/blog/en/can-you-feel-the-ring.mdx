## The short answer: placement, not the method

The vagina's lower third is where nerve endings that register touch and pressure are
concentrated. The upper two-thirds, where the ring sits when placed correctly, has far fewer.
That's the entire mechanism behind why most people don't feel it, and why the rare cases where
someone does feel it almost always come down to how far back it's pushed, not a fundamental
incompatibility with the method.

## If it feels noticeable to you

Try pushing the ring further back and up, similar to how a tampon is inserted past the point
where it would feel present. There's no single "correct" spot the way there is for a diaphragm;
the ring holds its position on its own once past the lower third, so slightly further back than
feels necessary is usually right.

If it's still noticeable after adjusting on a few different days, that's worth flagging to
whoever prescribed it. Vaginal length and shape vary, and a different ring size occasionally
makes a real difference.

## If your partner can feel it

Same underlying cause, same fix. Repositioning further back resolves this in most cases within
one or two cycles of experimenting with placement. It's not something either partner is doing
wrong: it's a mechanical placement question, not a compatibility one.

## You're allowed to remove it, briefly, if it matters to you

The ring can be taken out for up to 3 hours at a time, for any reason, including sex, without
needing backup contraception. Beyond 3 hours, or if you lose track of when you removed it, treat
it as a gap and use condoms until it's back in for at least 7 continuous hours.

The tradeoff most people land on: leave it in, since it genuinely isn't noticeable for most
people once placed correctly, and removing-and-remembering-to-reinsert is its own source of
missed-dose risk. But if you do prefer to remove it occasionally, the method supports that within
the 3-hour window.

## The actual risk isn't discomfort, it's forgetting to put it back

If you do remove the ring for a few hours, the timing detail that matters is remembering exactly
when you took it out and getting it back in before the 3-hour mark. That's a small, easy detail
to lose track of in the moment, which is where Loopa's reminder system is useful: log a temporary
