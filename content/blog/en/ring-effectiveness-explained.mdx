## Two numbers, one method

Every contraceptive method gets reported with two different effectiveness numbers, and mixing
them up is the single most common source of confusion when comparing methods.

- **Perfect use: 99.7%.** This is the effectiveness measured under ideal conditions, insertion
  and removal exactly on schedule, every single cycle, with no delays.
- **Typical use: around 91-93%.** This is the effectiveness measured across real populations of
  users, including everyone who occasionally inserts a day late or forgets which week they're in.

Both numbers describe the same ring. The difference isn't the hormone dose or the device, it's
how consistently the schedule was actually followed.

## Why the gap exists at all

The ring only requires 2 timed actions per month: one insertion, one removal (or immediate
reinsertion, for continuous use). Compared to a method requiring daily action, that's a small
number of opportunities to make a timing mistake, which is part of why the ring's typical-use
number tends to sit closer to its perfect-use number than some other methods.

But 2 actions per month, repeated across 12 months, still adds up to 24 timed events a year. A
missed or delayed one here and there, especially during travel or a disrupted routine, is enough
to pull the population-wide average down from 99.7% to the low 90s.

## What actually determines which number applies to you

The perfect-use number isn't reserved for a small group of unusually disciplined people. It's
simply what happens when the two monthly actions never slip. The typical-use number is what
happens, on average, when they sometimes do.

That means the deciding factor isn't willpower, it's whether you have a system that reliably
tracks the two dates that matter each cycle: when the ring goes in, and when it needs to come out
or be replaced. Miss that tracking and you drift toward the typical-use average. Get it right
consistently and there's no structural reason you can't sit closer to the perfect-use number.
